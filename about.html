About James Blackburn

Not a theologian.
Not a pastor.
Just someone who couldn't stop asking.

A mortgage guy from Fort Lauderdale who spent 26 years following a question — and ended up writing 1.26 million words about God, love, and the thread that connects everything.

Where it began

My father was twenty-two when his father died on a golf course. Heart attack. Gone.

He was already sick — Type 1 diabetes, the same disease that had slowly dismantled his father before him. His mother was a Christian Scientist who told him to pray instead of take insulin. The doctors said take the insulin or die. He chose the insulin. But I don't think he ever fully trusted either side.

When I was five, my mother left a note on the table and walked out. My father was sick, grieving his own father, and suddenly alone with a five-year-old and a two-year-old.

That's the wound I was born into. A father who didn't know who to trust. A mother who left when things got hard. And a child who learned early: people say I love you and then they leave.

Between my parents there were seven marriages total. The lesson I absorbed, without anyone teaching it to me: don't trust love. It doesn't last.

The wrong answer, a hundred times.

By twenty-two I had been with over a hundred women. Not bragging — documenting. Trying to fill the void my parents left when they kept leaving.

I wasn't broken because I couldn't find love. I was broken because I couldn't receive it.

At nineteen, in a college philosophy class, a professor asked a question that stopped me cold: Is love voluntary? That question hung unanswered for twenty-six years.

The turn

I married a woman for a green card. Three weeks after we met.

Twenty-two years later I'm still married to her. And at forty, when I held my daughter for the first time — my whole body shaking, crying harder than I'd ever cried — I understood for the first time what involuntary love actually feels like.

Love is involuntary. It's a program that just runs. And if God IS love — not has love, but literally IS love — then God's love works the same way. Automatic. Unconditional. Already running. Before you believe anything.

The Four Truths

Every teaching in The SonFlower and the Bear flows from four statements.

Truth 1
You are loved.
Not for what you do. For what you are.
Truth 2
You are not alone.
The thread cannot be severed.
Truth 3
You are not your worst moment.
Shame is not a prison.
Truth 4
It is never too late.
The door is always open.

The thread found him.
Now it's looking for you.

You just have to feel the ache. Find your door.
